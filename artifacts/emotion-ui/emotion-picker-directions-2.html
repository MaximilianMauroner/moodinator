Emotion step — 5 directions that maximise utility

These five are not density tweaks. Each one changes how you find the emotion you want among 40. Every frame is a true 411 × 820 dp viewport with the real default list and the real dark tokens. The frames are live: tap chips to select and deselect (limit 3), type in the search box, open the collapsed groups. Numbers in the tables are measured from the rendered DOM.

D learns from your history E lets you type F uses the mood you already picked G shows all 40 at once H sorts by energy instead of alphabet

Cost and risk, stated plainly

DirectionBuild costMain riskReversible?
D — Your usual One SQL query. mood_emotions already has idx_mood_emotions_emotion_id, so GROUP BY emotion_id ORDER BY COUNT(*) DESC LIMIT 8 is cheap. No schema change. A new user sees an empty row. Needs a fallback to the plain list until roughly 5 entries exist. Yes
E — Search first One TextInput and a filter. Also replaces the separate add-emotion modal (openCreateEmotionModal) with an inline create. The keyboard covers the lower half. Needs the list to shrink to the visible area, not scroll under it. Yes
F — Mood-aware Reuses getDefaultEmotionCategory(mood), which already exists at MoodEntryModal.tsx:98. Collapse state is local. Guessing wrong is a real cost. A person at mood 4 who feels anxious must open a group. Never hide a group that holds a current pick. Yes
G — Complete grid Layout only. No new data. Does not scale with custom emotions. Your device already has Calm, Optimistic and Dissatisfied on top of the defaults, and each addition erodes the no-scroll promise. 11.5 px labels also break under large system text. Yes
H — Energy bands Needs one static arousal: "high" | "mid" | "low" per emotion in domain/entrySettings.ts. 40 values, no DB migration. Custom emotions have no arousal value. They need a default band and a way to move between bands. Mostly

What I would ship

D plus E, in that order. They are the only two that get better the more you use the app, they solve different halves of the same problem, and they compose: the usual row covers the repeat case with zero effort, and search covers the long tail in about three keystrokes. Neither hides anything from you, which is the flaw in F and the risk in G. H is the most interesting of the five and the one I would keep for later, because it needs a new field and it helps most when you cannot name the feeling.
